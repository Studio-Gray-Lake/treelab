# TreeLab's foundations

What TreeLab is, what it holds to, and what it will not become. This is the page to read before deciding anything about it; [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) says how it is built.

## What TreeLab is

TreeLab grows trees and forests in a browser. You choose a kind of tree, an age, a place and a time of year, and it grows that tree at its real size, lights it with a real sun, and lets you walk the wood it stands in. What you make is yours to keep: a link that opens the same view for good, a picture, or a model to use anywhere.

It began as the forest for a game. It is now its own product: a tree and forest tool that others can pick up and use, and the game is one of its customers. Where the two pull apart, the product comes first.

## Its soul

**A tree is grown, not drawn.**

Nobody places a branch in TreeLab. A tree comes from how its kind grows, how old it is, where it stands and what time of year it is. An oak in the open is a broad dome; the same oak in a wood is a tall column with its leaves at the top; in mid winter it is bare but for a few brown leaves. Nothing was drawn three times: the same rules met three places.

That is the test for anything new. If it can be said as "a tree does this because of that", it belongs. If it can only be said as "we put this here because it looks good", it does not.

## The pillars

### 1. Grown from rules, and the same every time

- Every tree is the result of settings and a seed. The same settings and seed give the same tree, on any computer, today and next year.
- A slider changes what it names and nothing else. A taller oak is the same oak, taller. The seed is the one way to ask for another tree.
- Ready-made shapes, ages and seasons are places to start from. Each sets ordinary sliders in plain sight, and any of them can be moved by hand afterwards.

### 2. Nothing faked, and every shortcut said plainly

- Light, shade, mist and movement come from what is in the scene: the sun's real shadows, gaps that exist in the leaves, wind that the trees stand in.
- Where TreeLab simplifies, it says so, in the code and in the README, in the same words: "said plainly". Leaves are merged masses, not leaves. A hazel's stems all start on top of its stool. The finest twigs of a bare crown are not drawn.
- A shortcut is retired when the real thing is ready. It is never hidden.

### 3. Real trees, of real places, at their real size

- Each kind is a named species: the English oak, the small-leaved linden, the silver birch. It has the size, the habit and the bark that make it itself, in metres and years.
- What is said of a tree is checked against sources, and what could not be checked is marked as such.
- Kinds come in sets that belong together: the trees of one country and one age of the world make a wood that can be believed. The first full set is an English wood of the Middle Ages.

### 4. A tree answers to where and when it stands

- Age, neighbours, wind, ground and season all shape a tree. So each kind has more than one shape: in the open, in a wood, young, old, cut to the stool, bent by the wind.
- A forest is more than trees set side by side: each kind keeps to the ground it likes, trees keep the room their crowns need, and what grows under a crown is what bears its shade.
- The honest edge of this pillar: today a tree's shape does not yet answer to its neighbours by itself. Its wood-grown shape is chosen by hand. That is the direction, not yet the fact.

### 5. Something people can pick up

- It opens in a browser, with nothing to install, on one starting view that already looks like something.
- Every word a visitor meets is plain: the panel's rows, the tooltips, the notes. A name that can be misread is changed.
- A view is a link, and a link opens as it was made, always. What a link meant when it was made is kept for good.
- Sliders reach well past what is usual, so the outlying trees can be found.
- What you make is yours: exported models carry no restriction.

### 6. Light enough to run, and honest about the cost

- A desktop computer comes first. A recent phone is a check, not the target.
- Detail is spent where it shows. Every piece of work says what it costs in triangles, and says so when a wood gets much heavier.

### 7. Sober about itself

- Each piece of work ends with its weak points, listed beside what it does well.
- Asked how it compares, TreeLab answers straight. It is a stylised tree lab with a lit, walkable forest in a browser. It is not a production tool with scanned leaves and a library of hundreds of species, and it does not pretend to be.

## What TreeLab is not

- **Not a hand-modelling tool.** There is no brush and no pruning shears.
- **Not photographic.** Its leaves are masses and its wood is faceted, by choice. A leaf-by-leaf option may come; it will be an option.
- **Not a growth simulation.** Nothing seeds, competes or dies over years. A tree is grown whole, as it would stand at its age.
- **Not built to hook anyone.** It has no streaks, scores or nudges. If it is used, it is because it is useful or a pleasure to look at.
- **Not a game.** You can walk the wood; nothing happens to you in it.

## How decisions are made

- **Opinion first, then the work.** A request is answered with a view and its reasons before anything is built, and the weak points are named before they are found.
- **One step, one go-ahead.** Building, committing and publishing are separate steps, and each needs its own word. A yes to one is not a yes to the next.
- **Breadth before polish.** While a kind of tree reads as that tree, the next kind matters more than perfecting the last. What is set aside is written down so it can be picked up.
- **Decide by looking.** A change is shown as a picture beside what it replaces, at a known size, before it is called done.
- **Write it down where it will be found.** A decision goes in the code's own comments and the README, in the same plain words, not only in someone's memory.

## Open questions

These are not settled, and the pillars do not settle them.

- **How far the forest should grow itself.** The rules for where trees grow are rules of thumb. A wood that seeds, competes and thins over years would serve pillar 4 better and cost far more.
- **Leaves.** Merged masses are the default, and they limit how different two broadleaves can look from near. Sprays of leaves were tried and set aside as a later option.
- **Winter.** A bare crown needs finer twigs than a tree in leaf, and the ground does not yet change with the season.
- **What a paid version holds** that the free one in the browser does not.
